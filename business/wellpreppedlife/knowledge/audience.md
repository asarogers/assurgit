## Triggers — What Prompts the Search (depth: 0.65)

**[HIGH] Hospital discharge is the highest-urgency trigger: patients receive no actionable nutrition guidance at discharge**
`confidence: 0.91` `evidence: 2` `confirmed: 2026-04-05`
Hospital discharge is the highest-urgency trigger: patients receive no actionable nutrition guidance at discharge. Post-surgery, post-stroke, transplant, and CHF discharge packets mention "avoid raw foods" or give no guidance — creating a gap window where a one-time kitchen assessment has clear, immediate value. Target this trigger directly in content.

**[HIGH] Medication change is a reliable appetite-loss trigger: new prescription or dose increase → appetite drops within 2–4 weeks → caregiver doesn't connect the two for months**
`confidence: 0.89` `evidence: 3` `confirmed: 2026-04-05`
Medication change is a reliable appetite-loss trigger: new prescription or dose increase → appetite drops within 2–4 weeks → caregiver doesn't connect the two for months. Common culprits: metformin, SSRIs, digoxin, blood pressure meds, opioids. Content targeting this trigger should name the specific drugs to create recognition.

**[HIGH] Consistent unexplained weight loss despite normal food access is the conversion threshold trigger**
`confidence: 0.87` `evidence: 2` `confirmed: 2026-04-05`
Consistent unexplained weight loss despite normal food access is the conversion threshold trigger. When caregivers see weight loss they can't explain, they move from "figuring it out" to "I need professional help." This trigger is the primary CTA angle for the professional help threshold card (Card 25).


## Behavioral Patterns (depth: 0.65)

**[HIGH] "Figuring it out as I go" is the default caregiver behavior pattern — they research, adapt, and persist independently for months or years before seeking professional help**
`confidence: 0.90` `evidence: 4` `confirmed: 2026-04-05`
"Figuring it out as I go" is the default caregiver behavior pattern — they research, adapt, and persist independently for months or years before seeking professional help. The delay is not cost-sensitivity; it's the assumption that the problem is solvable with enough effort. Conversion requires demonstrating that the problem has a technical dimension that effort alone can't solve.

**[HIGH] Caregivers systematically fail to connect timing to causation: appetite changed months before they traced it to the medication change**
`confidence: 0.88` `evidence: 5` `confirmed: 2026-04-05`
Caregivers systematically fail to connect timing to causation: appetite changed months before they traced it to the medication change. Eating alone was misclassified as a logistics problem for 6 weeks. Poor sleep causing erratic appetite wasn't connected until a doctor mentioned it in passing. Content that explicitly names these hidden connections is the highest-value awareness play.

**[HIGH] Caregiver food fatigue is a real behavioral pattern: after 12–18 months, daily cooking becomes the most dreaded caregiving task**
`confidence: 0.86` `evidence: 2` `confirmed: 2026-04-05`
Caregiver food fatigue is a real behavioral pattern: after 12–18 months, daily cooking becomes the most dreaded caregiving task. At this stage, system reframes (freezer rotation, batch cooking) are more appealing than optimization of the existing approach. Target burnout-stage caregivers differently than early-stage caregivers.
